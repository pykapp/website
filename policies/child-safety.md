*people you know* is an app for adults. This page is the standard it holds
against child sexual abuse and exploitation, which Google Play requires every
app in its social category to publish. Ours is short, because most of what
such a standard usually has to describe cannot happen here by construction.

**Last updated:** 12 November 2026.

## The rule

Child sexual abuse material is forbidden here, as is any sexualisation of a
minor, and so is using the app to contact, groom or solicit a child. This is
not a matter of degree and there is no warning: an account that does any of
it is deleted rather than suspended, and reported.

## Nobody under eighteen

The app is for people aged eighteen and over. Signing up asks for a date of
birth before it asks for an address—deliberately in that order, so that
somebody turned away at the door has not handed over a verified address for
nothing—and an account that does not attest to eighteen is never created. We
keep the moment the gate was passed and not the date that was typed.

## Strangers have almost no way in

Most child safety work on a social app is about strangers reaching children.
This app gives a stranger almost nothing to reach anybody with:

- There are no public profiles, no directory, and no search by name. Somebody
  can find you only by typing your exact handle, email address or phone
  number.
- Somebody who finds you that way can ask to connect. Somebody who knows your
  email address can also invite you to a shared album they made. A request
  carries no message, and nothing is shared until you accept it. In settings
  you can limit requests to people who share a mutual connection with you.
- Every connection is mutual, both people agree to it, and you can have a
  hundred and twenty-eight at most. There are no followers and no audience to
  build.
- Nothing is recommended, suggested, ranked or inserted into your feed.
- There are no private messages. Only the people you are connected to can
  post to you, and only they can write a comment you will see. The one
  exception is a shared album. Whoever makes an album can put their own
  connections in it without asking them, and invite anybody by email address.
  Everybody in an album sees what the others post there, and can comment,
  react and mention the others, including people they are not connected to.
  You can leave an album at any time. To block somebody in it, tap their name,
  then **more** and **block**.

## What we can and cannot see

The app is end to end encrypted. Photographs, captions, comments and names are
encrypted on the phone that made them, with keys we never see. We therefore
do not scan content, filter it, or review it proactively, and we cannot: there
is nothing for a scanner to read. Our [moderation policy](/moderation/) says
the same at greater length.

We say this plainly rather than implying a capability we do not have. What it
means in practice is that our child safety work is enforcement on reports and
on accounts, and prevention by design, rather than detection in content.

## Reporting

**In the app.** Tap **more**, then **report**, on a post, on a person's
profile, or on a request somebody sends you. For somebody you are not
connected to, such as somebody in a shared album with you, tap their name,
then **more** and **report** on the dialog that opens. A comment has no
**report** of its own: report the post it is under and say which comment, or
tap the name of the person who wrote it. The report tells us which post or
person it is about and, for a post, whose it is. Unlike your posts, captions
and comments, what you type in the report is not end to end encrypted, so we
can read it.

The person reported is never told who filed it or what it said. If we remove
one of their posts and their account has an email address, we email them that
it was removed after somebody asked. The email does not say who
asked or why. We send nothing to an account we delete for child sexual abuse
or exploitation.

**By email**, if it needs a person rather than a button, or if you are not a
user of the app: pykapp+safety@proton.me.

Because we never receive a copy of the content, please say in words what is
wrong. It is the one part of a report we can read.

## What happens then

An account we find to be engaged in child sexual abuse or exploitation is
deleted, which severs every connection it has and removes its posts and
comments for everybody at once. Before we delete it, we keep a copy of the
records we hold about it:

- its email address and phone number, if it had them;
- each handle we have recorded for it, and when it had each one;
- its two public keys;
- when it joined, and when it said it was eighteen or over;
- when we suspended it, if it was already suspended for something else;
- each phone it was signed in on, with that phone's push token and the
  version of the app on it;
- each time it signed in, when that sign-in was last used, and when it signed
  out;
- who it was connected to, and since when;
- when it posted, how many photos and videos each post held, and to whom;
- the subscription it paid through, if any, by the store's reference for it,
  and when the period paid for ends;
- for each emailed invitation to an album that it accepted, when the
  invitation was sent and when it accepted;
- what the report we acted on said or, for a report by email, our note of it.

We report it to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC)
and to law enforcement in the jurisdictions that apply to us. We keep those
records for a year from our report, as United States law requires, and then
we delete them. We comply with lawful requests from those authorities.
